# Document Purpose

The Master Research Document (MRD) is the primary scientific record of the Global One Health Intelligence Framework (GOHIF).

Unlike the Master Dataset Inventory (MDI), which catalogs datasets, and the Master Development Document (MDD), which describes computational implementation, the MRD documents the scientific rationale, methodology, evidence evaluation strategy, and research decisions that guide the project.

The MRD serves as the scientific foundation for future publications and ensures complete reproducibility of research decisions.

---

# Project Vision

Develop a reproducible, explainable, evidence-driven computational framework that integrates heterogeneous One Health evidence for antimicrobial resistance (AMR) surveillance, evidence prioritization, hypothesis generation, and decision support.

---

# Frozen Research Gap

Current AMR resources provide valuable surveillance data but no existing computational framework simultaneously:

- Integrates heterogeneous One Health evidence
- Preserves evidence provenance
- Evaluates evidence quality
- Computes evidence confidence
- Harmonizes multi-domain evidence
- Provides explainable prioritization
- Supports transparent hypothesis generation

GOHIF addresses this gap through a unified evidence-centric architecture.

---

# Scientific Objectives

1. Build a unified One Health evidence framework.

2. Harmonize heterogeneous evidence.

3. Preserve evidence provenance.

4. Assess evidence quality.

5. Develop an evidence confidence scoring framework.

6. Implement explainable evidence prioritization.

7. Support transparent hypothesis generation.

8. Build an extensible computational framework for decision support.

# Phase 2 Scientific Methodology

The objective of Phase 2 is to construct a scientifically rigorous evidence foundation before computational framework development begins.

No machine learning, statistical modelling, or AI methods are implemented during this phase unless required for methodological evaluation.

Evidence acquisition follows a structured evaluation workflow.

Dataset Discovery

↓

Literature Review

↓

Metadata Assessment

↓

Scientific Quality Assessment

↓

FAIR Assessment

↓

Evidence Classification

↓

Acceptance Decision

↓

MDI Registration

↓

Freeze

# Dataset Selection Principles

Human Health datasets were evaluated according to the following criteria.

- Scientific authority

- International recognition

- Metadata quality

- FAIR maturity

- Provenance transparency

- Community adoption

- Sustainability

- Licensing

- Reproducibility

- One Health relevance

These criteria remain fixed throughout GOHIF.
